Production tips:
- Record screens at 1080p, one take per section, then cut.
- Use the seeded run that guarantees the "Certain and wrong" moment.
- Put the odds table on screen for 2 seconds near the bet (it shows we are transparent).
- Keep the voiceover calm and a little dry, in The Collector's spirit.
- Add captions. Many judges watch muted.

## 5. Likely judge questions and good answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why not just ask ChatGPT for a quiz? | A chatbot gives you questions. It doesn't track whether you were sure and wrong, schedule retests of your confident mistakes, or plan around your exam date. The bet is what exposes the gap |
| Isn't this gambling? | Australia's classification test for simulated gambling looks for a stake, a randomised outcome and a possible loss. Our chance devices never take a stake: the Prize Wheel only spins after a ball is caught, has no zero and only adds chips. There's no real money, no purchasable currency and no cash-out, chips never go negative, and the odds are printed before every chance event. We reskinned the old Roulette and Blackjack pegs as game show props, there are no near-miss effects, and in Calm mode every chance device just pays its average. At least 80% of an honest player's chips come from knowing things, and the Shift summary shows that skill share. |
| Can't I just claim Certain every time? | Wrong at Certain adds 12 Ledger debt against 6 chips for being right, so it only pays above about 83% sure. The tests prove an overclaimer earns less than an honest player at the same accuracy, at every Stake. Debt never takes your chips, but it shrinks your Ledger Pot payout and shows on Exam Day. |
| What keeps people coming back without dark patterns? | Prices rise with what you buy, not what you earn. After Exam Day you turn learning (never chips or time) into Mastery Marks for relics that carry into the next unit. No login streaks, no timed offers, achievements only for learning. |
| Does it actually work? | The methods behind it (retrieval practice, retests of confident errors) have strong research support. The combination is new, and one study of confidence-based marking found no improvement in exam scores, so we plan to measure outcomes in a pilot rather than assume them |
| What about wrong AI questions? | Generated questions are checked by an independent solve pass and the demo unit is hand-verified. Every question has a flag button and flagged questions drop out |
| How does it make money? | Free core loop. An Exam Season Pass and yearly plan, plus course dashboards for lecturers later. No ads and no selling chips |
| Who is it for? | University students facing exams, starting with units that have multiple-choice or short-answer assessments |
| Is the readiness score a grade prediction? | No. It's an estimate with the number of questions shown, and we say so |
| What did you build in the time? | The full loop on three hand-checked offline units, live generation with an independent check and a fallback, a crazy shop of board pegs, streaks, two skins and a mascot, with 45 automated tests including an economy tuning sim |
| Why will people keep playing? | Cookie Clicker style progression is next: shop prices rise as you earn, so there is always something to save for, and the fastest way to earn is to know more and bet honestly |
| Isn't Gizmo already a gamified study app? | Yes, and it shows students like game mechanics. Gizmo gamifies habits with streaks and lives. In Sure Thing the game mechanic is the learning method, and we never penalise wrong answers with lockouts |
| Did you copy John Gleep? | We took inspiration from the structure (debt, pegs you customise, upgrades). The theme, characters, art and audio are our own |

## 6. Submission text draft (Devpost style)

**Inspiration.** Every student has walked into an exam feeling ready and been wrong. Rereading feels like learning, but it isn't, and nobody knows what they are fooling themselves about.

**What it does.** Sure Thing turns revision into a plinko roguelike where every concept in your unit is a peg. Before each answer you bet on how sure you are. Right and confident pays well, wrong and confident plants a bomb on that topic. A debt countdown to your exam date drives what you study next, and a readiness report shows your confidence gap and tonight's plan.

**How we built it.** Next.js, TypeScript and Matter.js for the board. Game maths (scoring, spaced retests, calibration) are deterministic code. The model writes and verifies questions with an independent solve check, and the demo unit is hand-verified with an offline fallback.

**Challenges.** Making honest confidence the winning strategy (we tuned the payout so Guess, Pretty sure and Certain are each optimal at different levels of knowledge), keeping the game from feeling like a casino, and making AI questions reliable.

**Accomplishments.** A complete loop from setup to readiness report, a board that doubles as a feedback screen, and safe-play controls built in from day one.

**What we learned.** The research on calibration and confident errors is rich and sometimes contested, so we label outputs as estimates and plan to measure outcomes.

**What's next.** Pilot with one course, a class insight dashboard for lecturers, and a teach-it-back round where you explain a concept to an AI student.

## 8. If something breaks

| Failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Network down | Use offline mode (seeded bank). The app should never need the network for the demo |
| Board lags | Hit Skip animation, mention the physics and move on |
| Wrong AI question appears | Use the flag button live. It becomes a talking point about quality control |
| Judge asks about gambling harm | Lead with the guardrails, acknowledge the risk honestly, mention the 18+ plan for public release |
| Video fails | Play the backup recording and narrate |
